# Illustration & pattern

BESOQ does not use illustration as a marketing surface. There are no mascots, no decorative graphics, no editorial illustrations next to articles. There are, however, three permitted forms of mark-making, defined narrowly here.

---

## 1. Technical line drawings (the only "illustration" the brand makes)

A technical line drawing is the brand's preferred way to *show* a garment without photographing it. It appears on:

- Care cards (a small line drawing of the garment).
- Spec sheets (the working drawing).
- Repair service forms (the customer marks the area to be repaired).
- The back of a hangtag (subtle, embossed).

### Construction rules
- Pure line, single weight (`0.5pt` print / `1px` web).
- Drawn straight in vector — no hand wobble, no organic line.
- Black on bone, or bone on ash. No colour.
- Front view; back view; flat lay. Never perspective, never isometric.
- No interior shading, hatching, or detail beyond construction lines.

### Where they live

Files: `brand/assets/illustrations/` — one SVG per garment, named to match the SKU slug.

---

## 2. Patterns

The brand has **two** patterns. Both are tonal — same colour family, low contrast — and used quietly.

### Pattern 1 — `bsq` jacquard

The monogram `bsq` repeated in a precisely-spaced grid. Used as a tonal jacquard for garment linings, the inside of accessory leather goods, and the interior of premium packaging.

**Rules.**
- Monogram set at 8mm height.
- Repeat: every 24mm horizontally, every 32mm vertically (offset on alternating rows).
- Contrast: tonal only — same colour, different finish (matte vs. satin in jacquard, deboss vs. flat in leather).
- **Never** used at high contrast.
- **Never** used on outer garment surfaces.

```
  bsq      bsq      bsq
       bsq      bsq      bsq
  bsq      bsq      bsq
       bsq      bsq      bsq
```

### Pattern 2 — The bone-on-bone weave

A near-invisible woven texture used as a paper finish on bone-stock packaging. It is not a printed pattern; it is a paper choice. The "pattern" is the weave of the paper itself, visible at close range, invisible at distance.

This is the brand's preferred surface treatment for premium packaging.

---

## 3. Diagrams (the third permitted graphic)

Where a piece of information is best shown as a diagram (a size guide, a fabric structure, a layout grid), the diagram follows strict rules:

- Single line weight (`0.5pt` print / `1px` web).
- Sans-serif labels (Söhne) at 9pt minimum.
- Ash on bone. No colour, no fill.
- Right angles wherever possible. No curved arrows.
- Annotations sit outside the diagram, never inside it.

Diagrams in this brand kit (the file you are reading is one of many) follow this style.

---

## Forbidden mark-making

- ❌ Hand-drawn illustration of any kind. The brand does not "illustrate."
- ❌ Watercolour or painterly textures.
- ❌ "Hand-lettered" type — including the wordmark.
- ❌ Decorative borders, ornaments, dingbats, fleurons.
- ❌ Mascots — animal, human, abstract, or otherwise.
- ❌ Spot illustrations next to text (e.g., a pen icon beside "edit").
- ❌ Patterns with multiple colours.
- ❌ Patterns that imitate other brands' signatures (gingham, monogram canvas, houndstooth).
- ❌ Generative or AI-output imagery on customer-facing surfaces.

---

## Why the discipline is so strict

Illustration is the easiest place for a quiet brand to become decorative. A small spot illustration here, a pattern there, a watercolour wash on a campaign — and within a season, the brand has become a different brand. The ban on illustration is not aesthetic preference. It is structural protection.

The system is, by design, **photography + type + cloth**. That is the brand's full visual vocabulary. The technical drawings, the bsq jacquard, and the diagrams exist within that, never beside it.
