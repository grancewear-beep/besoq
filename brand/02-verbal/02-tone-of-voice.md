# Tone of voice

The way BESOQ writes is as designed as the way BESOQ tailors. This document is the canonical reference for every word the brand publishes — product copy, emails, social, customer care, press, packaging, signage.

---

## Voice in one sentence

> Calm, direct, lowercase. Speaks like a maker, not a marketer.

---

## The four voice attributes

### 1. **Quiet**
We do not raise our volume to be heard. We trust that the right reader is paying attention.

> ✅ the coat is back. eight years in the making.
> ❌ HUGE NEWS!! The coat you've been waiting for is FINALLY HERE!!

### 2. **Direct**
We say what we mean in the fewest words. We do not pad. We do not soften with corporate cushioning.

> ✅ this run is small. when it sells out, it is gone for the season.
> ❌ we wanted to let you know that, while supplies last, you may want to consider...

### 3. **Material**
We talk about cloth, weight, hand, and construction. We do not talk in vibes.

> ✅ a 320 gsm wool melton, woven in biella, brushed twice.
> ❌ a luxurious, premium fabric that feels amazing.

### 4. **Trusting**
We speak to a reader who is intelligent and busy. We do not over-explain. We do not flatter.

> ✅ size up if you prefer the boxy fit.
> ❌ you, our incredible customer, deserve to know that for the very best fit...

---

## Voice rules — non-negotiable

1. **Lowercase by default.** All marketing, social, product, packaging, and email body copy is set in lowercase. Exceptions: legal documents, press releases, and proper nouns (a city, a person, a mill).
2. **No exclamation marks.** Ever. Not once. Not even in customer service. Surprise is communicated through information, not punctuation.
3. **No emoji.** Not in social, not in email, not in product copy.
4. **No hype words.** Banned: *iconic, must-have, drop, exclusive, limited edition, luxury, premium, amazing, incredible, perfect, ultimate, game-changing, revolutionary, curated.* (See full list in `06-glossary.md`.)
5. **Em dash, not en dash, not hyphen.** Used for breath and qualification — like this — and around line names: `besoq — spring 26`.
6. **Numerals as digits.** "8 buttons," not "eight buttons." Exception: the number opens a sentence, in which case rewrite the sentence.
7. **Periods on standalone lines.** Even one-word lines end in a period. `wool. cotton. linen.`
8. **Active voice.** "we wove this in biella" — not "this was woven in biella."
9. **First person plural.** The brand is "we," never "BESOQ" in body copy.
10. **No questions in headlines.** Headlines state. Questions are lazy.

## What we sound like (annotated examples)

### Product page — coat

```
the long coat
besoq — autumn 26

a 380 gsm pressed wool from biella, in stone.
unlined for movement. four buttons. no logo, inside or out.

made in portugal, in a small atelier we have worked with for two seasons.

— 380 gsm wool, 100%
— horn buttons
— shoulder built without padding
— hand-finished cuff
```

Note: no adjectives of approval ("beautiful," "stunning"). Facts only. The reader judges.

### Email — collection launch

```
subject: autumn 26 — the long coat

eleven months of fittings. one cloth. one cut.
on site now.

— besoq
```

Note: subject line states without enthusiasm. Body is three lines. Sign-off is em dash + lowercase wordmark.

### Customer care — out of stock

```
the boxy shirt in stone is sold out for the season.
it returns in early spring as part of the permanent line.
if you would like to be told when it is back, leave your email here:
[link]

— ayla, besoq care
```

Note: customer care uses full sentences and a first name. Slightly warmer than marketing; still no exclamation, still lowercase.

### Social — instagram caption

```
the long coat. autumn 26.
```

Note: that is the entire caption. Two fragments. One period each.

### Press release — launch announcement (uppercase exception)

```
BESOQ — AUTUMN/WINTER 2026

BESOQ presents its second collection, the autumn/winter 2026 line, releasing 14 september. The release centres on a single garment — the long coat — produced in a 380 gsm pressed wool from a Biella mill. The collection comprises twelve pieces.

— ENDS —
```

Note: press releases use sentence case for body but uppercase for the wordmark and section headers. This is the only context where uppercase is the default.

## Voice across the customer journey

| Stage | Tone variation |
|---|---|
| **Awareness** (campaigns, social) | Quietest. Often a single line per surface. |
| **Consideration** (PDPs, lookbooks) | Most material. Specifications, weights, mills. |
| **Purchase** (cart, checkout) | Most direct. Functional language only. No upsell. |
| **Confirmation** (order email) | Warm, brief. Acknowledge by first name if known. |
| **Care** (customer service) | Warmest. Full sentences, named representative, signed personally. |
| **Repair** (post-purchase) | Same as care. Add: gratitude that the customer kept the garment. |

The tone never crosses into casual ("hey there!") or corporate ("Dear valued customer,"). It stays in a narrow band between *quiet* and *warm-quiet*.

## Quick voice check (the three questions)

Before publishing any copy, ask:

1. **Could a luxury house with a quiet posture publish this?** If yes, ship.
2. **Could a fast-fashion brand publish this?** If yes, rewrite.
3. **Is there anything I can cut without losing meaning?** If yes, cut it.

If a sentence survives all three, it is BESOQ-voice.
