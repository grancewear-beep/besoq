# Social media

BESOQ is on social, but only on its own terms. The brand uses social as a quiet bulletin board, not as a content factory. This document defines exactly how each platform is used — and how it is not.

---

## Platform philosophy

> we publish less than every other brand in the category, and what we publish lasts longer.

The brand's posting cadence is **lower than industry standard by design**. Quality, restraint, and infrequency are the strategy.

---

## 1. Platforms used

| Platform | Use | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** | Primary. Product, material, atelier moments. | 2–4 posts per week, max. |
| **Pinterest** | Reference and material library only. | 1 board update per month. |
| **Are.na** | Studio-internal references, public-facing channel. | Curated continuously. |
| **YouTube** | Material story videos, repair-service explainers. Long-form, rare. | 2–4 uploads per year. |

### Platforms we do not use
- ❌ TikTok — the platform's velocity is incompatible with the brand.
- ❌ Twitter / X — short-form public commentary is not a brand surface.
- ❌ Facebook — for the same reason.
- ❌ LinkedIn — for the company page only, used minimally; never as a marketing surface.
- ❌ Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon — same reason as Twitter.

If a new platform emerges, the brand will not adopt it for at least 18 months. By then, its posture is clear.

---

## 2. Handle system

The handle is `@besoq` everywhere. If the handle is not available on a new platform, the brand will not be on that platform until the handle is acquired. There is no `@besoq.official`, no `@besoq.studio`, no `@besoq_az`, no `@thebesoq`.

---

## 3. Instagram — the operating manual

### Profile

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| Handle | `@besoq` |
| Display name | `besoq` |
| Bio | `be so quiet.` (one line) |
| Link | `besoq.com` |
| Profile picture | Bone tile with the wordmark `besoq` debossed-style, ash on bone. (See `brand/assets/social/profile.svg`.) |
| Highlights | None. Story highlights below the bio are forbidden. |

### Feed grid composition

The feed is composed deliberately, not posted reactively. Three rules:

1. **Three-image rhythm.** The grid alternates between *garment*, *material*, and *atmosphere* — in approximately a 3:2:1 ratio.
2. **Bone-tone consistency.** Every image's overall tone sits within the brand palette. Saturated, busy, or trend-chasing images are rejected before posting.
3. **Air on the grid.** Every fourth or fifth tile is a calm, near-monochrome composition (a wall, a pile of folded cloth, a hanger) — the grid's exhale.

### Image format

- Always 4:5 portrait. Never square. Never landscape.
- Edge-to-edge. No bone-on-bone borders to "frame" the image.
- No text overlays. No quote cards. No infographic posts.
- Watermark: none. The image is unstamped.

### Caption length

- Maximum: 2 lines.
- Most posts: 1 line.
- Sign-off: a separate line, `— besoq`, or none at all.

Examples:
```
the long coat. autumn 26.
```
```
380 gsm wool. biella. brushed twice.
```
```
fitting no. 11.
the shoulder, finally.

— besoq
```

### Hashtags

The brand uses **zero hashtags**. The reasoning:

- Hashtags are a discoverability tool. The brand is not chasing discoverability through hashtags.
- Hashtags introduce a tone (#OOTD, #fashion, #style) that conflicts with the brand voice.
- Hashtags are loud by definition.

If a campaign requires aggregation, customers can be invited to **tag** `@besoq`, not to use a brand hashtag. We do not invent `#BESOQwinter` — see `02-verbal/01-naming.md`.

### Reels & video

- Used rarely — fewer than 12 per year.
- Always silent (no music, no narration). The customer can scroll past without sound.
- Always slow — long held shots, no cuts faster than every 3 seconds.
- Never lip-syncs, trends, transitions, or "before/after" gimmicks.
- Maximum length: 12 seconds. Most are 6–8.

### Stories

- Used very sparingly — perhaps 1 story arc per week, often less.
- A story arc is at most 3 frames. Not 12. Not 18.
- No interactive stickers (polls, questions, sliders).
- No "swipe up" CTAs.
- No "ask me anything" sessions.
- No re-posts of customer photos with a "thank you" tile (see customer reposts below).

### Customer reposts

**Default: we do not repost customer photos.**

The brand's reasoning: encouraging customer reposts subtly shifts the relationship from "we make a garment, you wear it" to "we make a garment, you produce content for us." This is a quiet kind of extraction.

If a customer's photo is exceptional and they explicitly offer it, the brand may share it to **stories only** (never feed), with explicit credit and zero commentary. The story is shown for 24 hours. There is no "tag for a chance to be featured" prompt.

### Comments

- The brand's account does not reply to comments routinely.
- For customer-care issues raised in comments, the brand replies with a single line directing to `care@besoq.com`.
- The brand does not engage in comment-thread banter.
- The brand does not reply with emoji.
- Spam and abuse are deleted without comment.

### Following

The brand follows fewer than 60 accounts. The list includes:
- A small number of mills, ateliers, and material partners.
- A small number of architects, photographers, and studios whose work informs ours.
- Other quiet brands the team respects.

The brand does **not** follow:
- Other clothing brands at our price point (it reads as competitive watching).
- Influencers, including ones who wear our clothes.
- Press accounts (we have direct relationships with editors).

## 4. Pinterest — reference, not retail

Pinterest is used as a public extension of the studio's reference library. The brand maintains a small number of boards (≤ 8 at any time):

| Board | Content |
|---|---|
| `architecture` | Pawson, Zumthor, Studio Mumbai. |
| `material` | Cloth, weave, surface. |
| `garments — past` | Vintage references, archive clothes. |
| `proportion` | Studies of silhouette and cut. |
| `print & type` | Editorial layouts, book design. |
| `light` | Photographs of light, never of fashion. |
| `quiet rooms` | Interiors that feel like the brand. |
| `the wardrobe` | The brand's own product, separated by season. |

Pinterest is not used to drive traffic. It is curated like a library.

---

## 5. Are.na

Are.na is the brand's most honest social platform — closest to how the studio actually thinks. The brand maintains 3–5 channels publicly. They include studio references, fabric ideas, and editorial direction. The voice is the same as everywhere else.

---

## 6. YouTube

A small channel with 2–4 videos per year. Videos are:

- 4–8 minutes long.
- Single static or slow-tracking shot.
- Voice-over only when necessary; otherwise silent or with ambient sound.
- Never about the founder; always about the cloth, the garment, or the repair.

Examples:
- "the long coat — eleven months."
- "the boxy shirt — placket."
- "repair — first 50."

The channel has no end-cards, no subscribe animations, no "smash that bell" anywhere. The video ends and the screen fades to bone.

---

## 7. Influencers, gifting, partnerships

The brand's posture on influencer marketing is clear:

- ❌ No paid influencer partnerships.
- ❌ No gifting "in exchange for a post."
- ❌ No discount codes for creators.
- ❌ No affiliate programme.

The brand does **gift** in two narrow contexts:
1. To people whose work the team genuinely admires (architects, writers, mill operators) — with no expectation, no agreement, no follow-up.
2. To press and stylists for editorial coverage — with a single A6 thank-you note, no terms attached.

If a recipient chooses to wear the garment publicly, the brand may quietly acknowledge it from the founder's personal account if appropriate, but never reposts it as marketing.

---

## 8. Crisis & negative content

If a customer posts publicly about a defective garment, the response is:

1. Reply once, in a single line: "we are sorry. please write to care@besoq.com — we will solve this."
2. Take the conversation off-platform.
3. Do not delete the original post (the brand does not police criticism).
4. Resolve the issue genuinely.
5. Add the case (anonymised) to the internal "what we got wrong" log.

If misinformation circulates about the brand, the response is a single quiet correction on the brand's owned channels, not a public argument.

---

## 9. Forbidden social behaviours

- ❌ "Drop reminders" on social.
- ❌ Shop-the-look stickers on every story.
- ❌ Founder-led "personal" content.
- ❌ Trend participation (challenges, formats, sounds).
- ❌ Engagement-bait questions ("which one is your favourite?").
- ❌ Comment-section community-building under posts.
- ❌ Rushed posting to "stay relevant."
- ❌ Apologising for a quiet posting cadence ("sorry we've been quiet — busy season!"). The cadence is the brand.

---

## 10. The single content test

Before any social post is published, one question:

> **could this image, with this caption, in this length, exist on the wall of the store?**

If yes, post.
If no, don't.

The store and the social are the same room. The format is just different.
