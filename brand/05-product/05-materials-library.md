# Materials library

The brand's working library of cloths, with sourcing, weight, behaviour, and approval status. This is a living document. Materials are added by the studio after a sample-and-test process; they are removed only by formal retirement.

---

## Why a library

Most clothing brands change cloth seasonally and publicly emphasise the change ("new for spring"). BESOQ does the opposite: a small library of trusted cloths is reused across seasons, reused across years, and only expanded slowly.

This library is what allows the brand to keep a permanent line stable for years. A garment that re-uses a known cloth can be reproduced exactly, season after season, without drift.

## 2. Material status taxonomy

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sample** | Cloth is being tested; not in production. |
| **Approved — permanent** | Cleared for permanent line use; reorderable indefinitely. |
| **Approved — primary** | Cleared for any line; primary cloth in its category. |
| **Approved — premium** | Cleared, reserved for higher-cost garments. |
| **Approved — seasonal** | Approved for a specific season; not held year-round. |
| **Sunset** | No longer ordered; existing stock used through. |
| **Retired** | Permanently retired; not used again. |

## 3. Adding a new material — the process

A new cloth enters the library only after passing this five-step process. **No exceptions.**

### Step 1 — Mill selection
The mill is chosen for craft, traceability, and willingness to share specifications. Mills that won't disclose origin are rejected.

### Step 2 — Sample test
A 50cm sample is acquired. The studio tests:
- Weight (independently measured).
- Shrinkage after wash.
- Hand after 5, 10, and 20 washes.
- Drape on the body.
- Pilling resistance.

### Step 3 — Garment prototype
The cloth is made into a single garment using an existing pattern block. The garment is worn for 4 weeks by a member of the studio. Daily notes are kept.

### Step 4 — Production trial
A small production run (≤ 30 garments) is produced and offered as part of a Study (`besoq — study no. NN`). Customer feedback is collected.

### Step 5 — Library entry
If the cloth passes Steps 1–4, it is added to the library with a unique `M-NNN` ID. The mill, mill operator, and origin are recorded — and disclosed publicly.

### Steps that do NOT enter the library
- ❌ "Trend" cloths that are not yet stable.
- ❌ Cloths whose mill has not been visited in person at least once.
- ❌ Cloths without a defensible composition disclosure.
- ❌ Cloths that contain "RPET" or post-consumer plastic. (See section below.)

## 4. Materials NOT used

The brand has firm positions against certain materials.

### Forbidden categories
- ❌ **Virgin polyester / nylon** — landfill in 50 years; unrepairable.
- ❌ **Recycled polyester (RPET)** — sheds microplastics; sustainability claim that is largely invalid for clothing.
- ❌ **Conventional leather** — only vegetable-tanned leather from named tanneries is permitted; in practice, the brand uses very little leather.
- ❌ **Mulesed wool** — non-mulesed certification required.
- ❌ **Animal fur** — categorically.
- ❌ **Exotic skins** — categorically.
- ❌ **Lyocell / modal blends labelled "natural"** — used only as 100% lyocell from named producers (Lenzing) with full traceability.
- ❌ **"Performance fabrics"** — moisture-wicking, anti-microbial, etc. The brand does not make activewear.

### Used cautiously
- **Cashmere** — only with a verified, named, single-flock supply. The brand does not use mass-blended cashmere.
- **Silk** — only Ahimsa (peace) silk, named producer.
- **Down** — only Responsible Down Standard certified, named supply.
- **Cotton** — preferred organic; conventional accepted only when the mill discloses provenance.

## 5. Disclosure on the product page

Every PDP discloses the cloth at three levels of detail:

### Level 1 (always shown)
```
cloth     380 gsm wool, 100%
mill      [mill name], biella
make      portugal
```

### Level 2 (one click, "more about the cloth")
A short paragraph about the cloth's behaviour and history.

### Level 3 (linked from L2, "the mill")
A page in `/materials/` describing the mill, its location, its house, and a photograph or two.

The level of detail is the brand's commitment to Pillar 4 (Honest).

## 6. Material identifiers in SKUs

Each cloth has its `M-NNN` ID, but the SKU does not embed it. Cloth provenance is tracked in the registry file `product-data/cloth.yaml`, mapped to garment SKUs there.

This separation matters because **the same garment may use different cloths across revisions**. The boxy shirt at `r1` may use M-001; at `r3` it may use a slightly heavier M-001-2. The SKU evolves; the cloth registry tracks which cloth is at which revision.

## 7. Cloth holds & order discipline

The brand orders cloth in advance of production, not on demand. A typical cadence:

- **18 months out** — cloth options sampled.
- **12 months out** — cloth ordered for the season.
- **6 months out** — cloth received at the maker.
- **3 months out** — production begins.
- **Release** — garments ship.

The discipline is operational, not promotional. The brand does not advertise its production timeline as a feature.

---

## 8. Cloth retirement

A cloth is retired when:
- The mill discontinues it.
- The hand or weight changes between batches by more than 5%.
- The mill closes.
- The studio discovers a defect across a production run.

Retiring a cloth is a serious event. Garments that depended on it may be re-cut in a replacement cloth, re-tested, and re-released as a new revision (`r2`). The brand does not silently substitute.

---

## 9. Forbidden material behaviours

- ❌ Marketing a cloth as "luxurious" without weight and mill.
- ❌ Naming a private cloth ("the BESOQ Bone Wool"). Mills are named, not branded.
- ❌ Applying a "BESOQ" name to a third-party material.
- ❌ Re-issuing a retired cloth without testing.
- ❌ Mixing fibre families (cotton + polyester) without explicit composition disclosure.
- ❌ Using a "blend" to hide a synthetic content above 5%.
- ❌ Holding cloth back from the registry to "save it" for a special collection. Every cloth is documented when adopted.
