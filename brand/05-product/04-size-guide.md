# Size guide

The size guide is, on most brand sites, an afterthought — a help-page nobody reads carefully. BESOQ treats it as a primary product surface. Sizing is the largest source of return-related friction in apparel; getting it right is a brand virtue, not a UX problem.

---

## Philosophy

> if a customer is not sure of their size, the brand has not communicated enough.

The brand does not punish customers for sizing uncertainty. The size guide is direct, complete, and supplemented by per-garment notes.

---

## 1. Size systems used

| System | Used for |
|---|---|
| **Letter sizes** (`XS, S, M, L, XL`) | Tops, knitwear, outerwear |
| **Numbered sizes** (`44, 46, 48, 50, 52, 54`) | Tailoring (trousers, suits) |
| **Knitwear scale** (`KN1, KN2, KN3, KN4`) | Some heavy hand-knit pieces |
| **One size** (`OS`) | A small, named subset of intentionally-loose pieces |

The brand does not use:
- ❌ "S/M" or "L/XL" combined sizes.
- ❌ Children's sizing (the brand does not make kidswear).
- ❌ Petite or tall ranges as separate categories — see "Cut variants" below.
- ❌ "European" vs. "US" duplicate sizing (we use a single global system).

## 3. Garment measurement chart (the precise one)

For each garment, the **garment-flat-measured** dimensions are also published. This is more useful than body measurements because it lets the customer compare to a garment they already own.

### Example: the boxy shirt

| Size | Chest (flat × 2) | Shoulder | Sleeve | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **XS** | 116 | 50 | 58 | 70 |
| **S** | 122 | 52 | 60 | 72 |
| **M** | 128 | 54 | 62 | 74 |
| **L** | 134 | 56 | 64 | 76 |
| **XL** | 140 | 58 | 66 | 78 |

The "boxy" cut is reflected in the chest-flat number. A customer with a 100cm chest is looking at a 128cm garment-flat in size M — the gap (28cm) is the brand's shoulder line.

Each garment on the website carries this table on its product page.

## 4. Per-garment fit notes

In addition to numbers, every garment has a **fit note** — a single human paragraph describing how the cut is intended to sit.

### Example — the boxy shirt

```
fit note
the boxy shirt is cut wide through the body and shoulder.
it sits straight, without a taper at the waist.
the model is 184 cm and wears a size m.
size up if you prefer the cut roomier.
```

### Example — the trouser, wide

```
fit note
the wide trouser sits at the natural waist.
the leg is straight from the hip; no taper at the ankle.
the inseam is 80 cm in regular, 86 cm in long.
the model is 178 cm and wears a size 48 in long.
```

The model's height is always disclosed. The size the model wears is always disclosed. We do not "fit-cheat" with a smaller model in a larger size.

---

## 5. Cut variants

For trousers and outerwear, the brand offers two inseam variants:

| Variant | Inseam | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Regular | 78–82 cm depending on size | Default. Most customers. |
| Long | 84–88 cm depending on size | Customers above ~182 cm. |

The brand does not call these "petite" and "tall" — terms that have acquired loaded marketing meanings. They are simply "regular" and "long."

There is no "short" variant — instead, regular trousers are designed to be tailored shorter at the studio (see `03-care-and-repair.md`).

## 7. Genderless approach

Most BESOQ garments are designed to be **genderless** — cut to a single block per size, sized to fit a wide range of bodies.

A subset of garments — primarily tailored pieces (the blazer, the long coat in some seasons) — is offered in two cuts: `cut a` (typically wider through chest, narrower through hip) and `cut b` (the inverse). These are not labeled "men's" or "women's." They are labeled by cut.

A customer chooses by body, not by gender. The PDP describes both cuts and lets the customer choose.

---

## 8. Sizing voice

Per `02-verbal/02-tone-of-voice.md`, the size guide is direct.

✅ "size m fits a 100 cm chest with room to spare."
❌ "Our M is *roomy* and *flattering* — perfect for the modern body."

The brand never describes sizes as "true to size," "runs small," or "runs large." These are imprecise. The brand publishes the numbers and lets the customer decide.

---

## 9. The personal sizing call

For a customer who is unsure, a single email can be sent to `care@besoq.com` requesting a sizing conversation. The customer-care team replies with a one-line offer:

```
ayla,

if you have ten minutes, we can talk through fit by email or video.
let us know which would be easier.

— sevda, besoq care
```

A 15-minute video call with a member of the customer-care team is offered free, with no upsell, no scripted sales flow. The team uses the customer's existing wardrobe (a shirt the customer owns and trusts) as the reference for the BESOQ equivalent.

This is not advertised on the homepage. It is a quiet offer surfaced to customers who ask. It is the brand's most direct expression of Pillar 4 (Honest).

## 11. Forbidden sizing behaviours

- ❌ Vanity sizing (labelling a 102cm chest as "M" because customers prefer smaller numbers).
- ❌ Inflated "fits S–L" claims for one-size pieces.
- ❌ Removing size availability from the PDP without explanation.
- ❌ "Sold out in your size" emails that link to a different size.
- ❌ "Customers like you wear size M" recommendations based on browsing data.
- ❌ Hiding garment-flat measurements behind a "show more" toggle.
- ❌ Using imperial measurements in primary copy. Imperial is offered as a secondary translation only, with metric authoritative.
