# Audience & personas

BESOQ does not target a demographic; it targets a posture. Three primary personas describe the bulk of the customer base. They are written as composites — not as marketing segments to be advertised at, but as people whose decisions guide our own.

---

## Persona 01 — The Returner

**Composite name.** Lara, 34. Lives in Berlin, Tbilisi, Lisbon, or any second city.

**Past.** Worked in branding, fashion, or tech in her twenties. Owned the loud bag, the logo coat, the sneaker drops. Recently donated most of it.

**Now.** Buys fewer pieces, more deliberately. Reads fabric composition before price. Has a small list of trusted brands and protects it.

**What she wants from BESOQ.**
- Garments she does not have to think about.
- Clothes that signal taste without naming it.
- The relief of not being marketed at.

**What turns her off instantly.**
- Influencer codes.
- "Drop" language.
- Founder photographs that try too hard.
- Any sentence containing the word "iconic."

**Her sentence about BESOQ (aspirational).**
"I have three of their shirts. I do not need a fourth this year."

---

## Persona 02 — The Builder

**Composite name.** Adil, 28. Architect, junior partner, freelance designer, or starting a studio.

**Past.** Grew up online, watched the streetwear era from inside it, saved hyped pieces for resale.

**Now.** Building a wardrobe deliberately, the way he builds a portfolio. Reads about cloth, asks about construction, photographs garments on a hanger before wearing them.

**What he wants from BESOQ.**
- A coherent system he can build on.
- Permanent pieces he can re-buy in the same fit.
- Information that respects his literacy.

**What turns him off instantly.**
- Vague descriptions ("premium fabric").
- Models that look 19 in catalogue shots.
- Sales he can predict.

**His sentence about BESOQ (aspirational).**
"They publish the mill. That tells me everything."

---

## Persona 03 — The Quiet Professional

**Composite name.** Nargiz, 45. Lawyer, professor, founder, or senior creative.

**Past.** Has worn tailoring her whole working life. Owns suits older than most brands.

**Now.** Wants modern wardrobe pieces that respect her standards but feel current. Refuses to dress younger than she is. Refuses to dress older.

**What she wants from BESOQ.**
- Cut that fits a real adult body.
- Fabrics that hold structure without rigidity.
- Garments that travel and recover.

**What turns her off instantly.**
- Crop lengths.
- Drop shoulders presented as tailoring.
- Branding visible from across a meeting room.

**Her sentence about BESOQ (aspirational).**
"Finally, a young brand I can wear to court."

## Anti-persona — who BESOQ is not for

We define this as carefully as the personas, because clarity about who we are not for protects who we are for.

- The customer who needs a brand to be recognised before they will buy it.
- The customer who measures value by discount.
- The customer who buys to post.
- The customer who treats clothing as currency rather than wardrobe.

We do not insult these customers. We simply do not design for them. They are well served by other brands.

---

## Persona use rules

1. Personas are decision filters, not marketing avatars. Do not advertise *at* them; design *for* them.
2. When a decision passes for two personas and fails for one, default to the failing persona — the question is usually whether the brand is drifting.
3. Never create a fourth persona to justify a product that fails the first three.
4. Personas are reviewed annually and may evolve, but new personas require an explicit pillar test (see `03-pillars.md`).
