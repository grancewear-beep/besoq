# Brand architecture

How the BESOQ name and identity are applied across products, lines, sub-brands, and partnerships.

---

## Architecture model: **branded house**

BESOQ uses a strict **branded house** model. The master brand (BESOQ) appears on every garment, line, and surface. There are no sub-brands with their own visual identities. Lines and capsules exist only as **descriptors under the master**.

This model was chosen because:
- The master brand's value is built on consistency.
- Sub-brands fragment attention, which conflicts with Pillar 1 (Quiet).
- A small brand cannot afford a portfolio.

---

## The line system

BESOQ operates four lines. All four are master-branded; none has its own logo or identity.

### 1. **Permanent**
The always-on core. Pieces designed to be re-bought, repaired, and kept across years. Restocked rather than redesigned. ~30 SKUs at maturity.

> Identifier: no suffix. The default state.

### 2. **Seasonal**
Two releases per year — **spring** and **autumn**. Each ~20 SKUs. Material and palette evolve with the season, but proportions inherit from Permanent.

> Identifier: `besoq — spring 26`, `besoq — autumn 26`.

### 3. **Archive**
Pieces from past Seasonal collections, sold at original price for as long as stock holds. Not discounted. When stock runs out, the piece is gone.

> Identifier: `besoq — archive`.

### 4. **Studies**
Limited, experimental pieces — usually one fabric or one silhouette explored in depth. ~3 to 6 SKUs per study, released irregularly. Numbered, not named.

> Identifier: `besoq — study no. 03`.

## Naming convention

| Element | Convention | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Master | `besoq` (lowercase, single word) | besoq |
| Line | em dash + line name in lowercase | besoq — spring 26 |
| Garment within line | line + simple descriptor | besoq — spring 26 — boxy shirt |
| Study | numbered, not named | besoq — study no. 03 |
| Collaboration | partner name placed second, equal weight | besoq × [partner] |

Spaces around the em dash are intentional. The em dash is non-negotiable; en dash and hyphen are not substitutes.

---

## Endorsement & co-branding

BESOQ collaborates rarely. When it does, the rule is:

1. **Equal weight, no merging.** `besoq × [partner]` — both names at the same size, same weight, no merged logo.
2. **One project, finite end.** No open-ended partner brands.
3. **Pillar test.** A collaboration must satisfy at least three of the four brand pillars. Two is a no.
4. **Quiet partners only.** We do not partner with brands whose voice would overwhelm ours.

---

## Forbidden architecture moves

- ❌ Sub-brands with their own logos (e.g., "BESOQ STUDIO" as a separate identity).
- ❌ Diffusion lines at lower price points.
- ❌ Licensed categories (e.g., BESOQ-branded eyewear made by a third party).
- ❌ Capsule collections used to disguise routine drops.
- ❌ "By BESOQ" endorsements on non-apparel.

## Future-state guardrails

If the brand grows beyond apparel (e.g., into homeware), the same architecture applies:

- The master brand `besoq` extends, not splits.
- Categories are descriptors: `besoq — home`, `besoq — leather`.
- The visual system is shared, with no category-specific deviation.

If, at any future point, the temptation to create a sub-brand emerges, it is a signal that the master brand is not strong enough to carry the new category — and the answer is to strengthen the master, not to fork.
